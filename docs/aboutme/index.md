Noah Nicholson

Originally from Massachusetts 45 mins from Boston. I loved racing from an early age in video games but I as I started watching motorsports I became quickly intrigued about how it all worked. I wanted to know how one driver could be so fast one week, but so slow the very next. I learned through broadcasts that most of the speed came not from the drive but from the engineers that build them. From there I learned through Iracing Simulations and NASCAR broadcasting that building racing setups was a balancing act of optimization to achieve the best competitive results. Noticing patterns in cross-weight in a race car and how to manipulate it to best suit a driver or track. During high school I built a Factory Five Racing Shelby Cobra kit car with my dad. This process of rigorously testing and test fitting pieces to the car just to take them off and modify them again gave me my first look into the mind and process of engineering. The level of detail needed to successfully understand and manipulate parts and pieces to fit our needs grew with every mistake and every installation. This understanding of trial, error, and development is what engineering is for me. The further I get into my degree, the more I realize that every aspect of engineering is at play and I learn to understand how all my classes at UNC Charlotte contribute and develop my understanding of mechanical engineering.

"What does it mean to defend an engineering decision : and do you currently know how to do it?"

To defend an engineering decision is to prove not only to yourself but to anyone that views your work, that it is an efficient and sound solution to the objective that is being achieved. To successfully defend an engineering decision is to have enough organized and supports evidence of your decision that there is no room for confusion on the matter from someone that is assessing the decision for the first time with no prior context.
